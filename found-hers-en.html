Foundhers was conceived to connect capital - grants, investors, businesses, individuals, whatever form it takes - to women building businesses that will feed their families, not chase a venture-scale exit. A funding model designed for the woman who needs this to work, not just to raise.

Why this exists

Most funding pipelines for women are built around pitch decks and growth curves. That works for a small slice of founders - and leaves out the far larger group of women who don't want to build the next unicorn. They want a shop, a service, a small manufacturing line: something that pays the bills, reliably, starting this year.

Foundhers was designed for that second group: women who have - or are ready to build - an income-generating business, matched with capital that isn't fussed about a venture-scale return - grant, investor, business, or individual money, whichever actually gets the business running.

The thinking traces back to a 2023 project submitted to UN Women's Women's Peace & Humanitarian Fund: an incubator taking women from idea, through training, to actually running and sustaining a business - not just completing a course.

Foundhers was drafted to carry that same shape forward for founders in the UK.

Proof the underlying model works

Foundhers is being built on a methodology that has already produced real, ongoing businesses - delivered through Ukrainian Sisters and its volunteers, funded by grants rather than by private investors so far.

How the model was designed to work

1

Intake & assessment

A founder's existing business - or business idea - is assessed against what it actually needs to become fundable: skills gaps, financial footing, and readiness.

2

Grounding & restoration

Before any business work starts, psychological support helps each woman find solid footing again - the personal resourcing that has to come before someone can build anything on top of it.

Carried directly from the 2023 model, where this came before any planning or training began.

3

Foundations

Structured mentoring sharpens the business model and turns a rough idea into a clear roadmap the founder can present with confidence.

4

Investment readiness

Practical modules - financial literacy, legal structure, marketing, day-to-day management - build the proposal up to something an investor can actually evaluate.

5

Capital matching

Curated introductions to the right source of capital for that business - grant, investor, business partner, or individual backer - with structured pitch sessions and transparent terms. No cold outreach, no guessing at fit.

6

Funded & sustained

Mentorship continues after the cheque clears. Funded founders are invited back as mentors - and, over time, as investors - for the next cohort.
